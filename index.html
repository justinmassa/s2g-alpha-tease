THE LENS

A theory of finance, not a theory of AI.

"The history of global markets is a series of jagged step-functions triggered by massive structural transitions. Alpha concentrates with the market builders who move early to deploy fit-for-purpose capital."
S2G Investments · Financing Reality · May 2026

S2G's argument, in one line: across 300 years of capitalism, alpha has accrued not to whoever built the technology but to whoever designed the capital architecture matched to the new economic reality, before conventional markets were equipped to underwrite it.

The joint-stock company preceded the East India trade it financed. The bond syndicate preceded the rail network it consolidated. The venture limited partnership preceded the semiconductor industry it capitalized. The structure arrives before the category it enables.

If that pattern holds, the question for generative AI is not "is it valuable?" (manifestly), but "where is the value that conventional capital cannot yet price, and what instrument must be built to price it?"

PREDETERMINED ELEMENTS

What is already locked in.

Six structural facts that hold across every scenario. The four futures diverge on what happens around them, not on whether they are true.

01 · COMPUTE

Hyperscaler capex is a freight train.

$400B in 2025 across MSFT, GOOG, META, AMZN, ORCL. Trending to $600-700B by 2027. Rubin and Rubin Ultra taped out. The compute is being built whether or not demand materializes; this is now a sunk-cost dynamic, not a demand-pull one.

02 · POWER

Power is the binding constraint, not chips.

US data center demand grows from 415 TWh (2024) to roughly 1,000 TWh by 2030. Interconnection queue at 2,300-2,600 GW. Gas turbines have 5-year lead times. SMRs are 2030+. Behind-the-meter generation is a predetermined winner.

03 · CAPITAL

The software premium has reset.

SaaS EV/Revenue compressed from 6.7x (2021) to 3.1x (Q1 2026). Per-seat subscription is being replaced by token, compute, and outcome billing. Thoma Bravo wound down its growth-equity strategy in April 2026. The mandate built for the last decade no longer fits.

04 · GEOPOLITICS

Partial bifurcation, not full decoupling.

US export controls commit to a permanent 2-3 process-node gap with China. ~90% of leading-edge logic fabricated within 180km of the Taiwan Strait. Gulf sovereign capital (G42, HUMAIN) emerges as a credible third pole by 2028.

05 · CLIMATE

2.5°C is the working assumption.

UN Environment Programme Emissions Gap 2025 puts end-of-century warming at 2.3-2.5°C under full pledges, 2.8°C under current policy. $746B of US weather/climate disaster losses 2020-24, 8x the 1980s baseline. AI infrastructure and physical risk collide.

06 · DEMOGRAPHICS

The headwind is set for the horizon.

Working-age populations contract. Agriculture's average operator is 58. One in three EU electrical engineers is 50+. Automation is being sold as the answer, but the buildout itself requires the labor the demographics already deny.

THE TWO AXES

What we don't know that matters most.

Six specialists (futurist, economist, geopolitician, anthropologist, ecologist, contrarian) converged on two uncertainties that split the future into genuinely different addresses for alpha.

The world in 2030.

By late 2029 the consensus AI trade has been vindicated in the most boring way possible. NVIDIA, Microsoft, Google, Meta, Amazon, Oracle, and TSMC have compounded earnings at 25 to 35% CAGR for six years. Hyperscaler capex peaked at $750B in 2027 and is plateauing at a $500B-per-year steady state. The interconnection queue is long but ERCOT, MISO South, and the Gulf states cleared enough power to feed the buildout. The IRA's transmission provisions worked. Three SMRs operate in Wyoming and Tennessee, twenty more under construction.

Frontier models continue to lead open-weight models by 12 to 18 months and that gap is stable. The four-firm foundation-lab oligopoly is durable. Application-layer companies live, die, and consolidate at 40%+ annual churn. A few breakouts reach scale; their margins are thin.

The physical-economy AI thesis quietly disappoints. Farmers do adopt yield-prediction and pest-detection vision systems, but the productivity gain is 3 to 6% per year, real but not transformative, and it diffuses to consumers via lower prices rather than to operators or investors.

What this means for S2G.

The scenario the Financing Reality report implicitly argues against. If it unfolds, the three-strategy framework still functions for existing sectors (food, energy, oceans) but the AI overlay does not produce the alpha S2G expects. The Special Opportunities strategy still works on its own merits. The Growth Equity strategy struggles. The Bespoke Funds strategy still earns its keep but does not benefit from an AI-specific tailwind.

"The consensus is correct, and S2G's relative advantage is in sector knowledge, not in capital-architecture differentiation. The alpha thesis dissolves into 'be a good sector specialist.'"

Panel weighting: ~25-30%. Plausible but not the central case. Requires friction to clear faster than the predetermined elements suggest, no Taiwan-class disruption, and the trust layer to form without institutional innovation.

The world in 2030.

The buildout stalls in slow motion. Through 2026 and 2027 the interconnection queue grows faster than it clears, by mid-2027 exceeding 3,200 GW with average waits past 8 years. Permitting reform is repeatedly attempted and repeatedly fails on coalition arithmetic. By 2028 the marginal hyperscale data center cannot site at scale anywhere in the continental US except a thin band of Texas, Wyoming, and the Mississippi Delta.

A Hormuz-style event in the Taiwan Strait in 2027 (short of war, but a six-week shipping disruption and a yearlong insurance premium repricing) reprices the entire chip supply chain. NVIDIA's GPU lead time stretches from 38 weeks to 70+ weeks. Allocation becomes overtly political. The Gulf states emerge as the third pole, not because of innovation but because they are the only place where 5 GW of new firm power can be permitted in under 18 months.

The EU AI Act enters full enforcement and bites harder than the megacaps expected. Two foundation labs suspend EU operations. Trust infrastructure forms, but as adversarial regulation rather than as market institution. AI notaries, model-audit firms, and AI insurance carriers become regulated industries, often state-affiliated.

Where the alpha lived

  • Permit-and-power assets in Texas, the Gulf, the UAE: utility-bond yields plus 10-15% scarcity premium
  • Behind-the-meter generation at hyperscale
  • Sovereign-affiliated infrastructure funds (ADQ, PIF)
  • Chokepoint owners: TSMC, ASML, Amkor, the CoWoS OSATs
  • The compliance-and-trust layer (state-affiliated but durable)
  • Talent monopsonists, the 4-5 firms that could afford $50-100M signing packages

Where the alpha did NOT live

  • The application layer; margins reprice to whatever the model charges
  • Mid-tier neoclouds (acquired or financialized into compliance utilities)
  • Generalist AI funds; cannot access rationed compute, cannot buy chokepoint assets at reasonable price
  • Most physical-economy AI deployments; the buildout was too slow to penetrate the demographic transition

What this means for S2G.

Mixed. S2G is well-positioned in energy infrastructure and benefits from the energy-AI nexus repricing. The Mitra-EV, ANA-Energy, Echandia portfolio reads correctly here. Special Opportunities (structured finance against behind-the-meter assets) is highly relevant.

S2G is poorly positioned to capture the full alpha because the biggest pools (sovereign capital and chokepoint equity) are not where their LP base sits. The market-builder narrative gets a partial vindication: capital structure matters, but mostly as a way to get exposure to the rentier assets, not as a way to build new categories.

"The alpha is mostly in the assets themselves. The capital architecture is the wrapper, not the prize."

Panel weighting: ~30-35%. The modal scenario. Predetermined elements (power constraint, partial bifurcation, demographic crunch, climate physical risk) are all in motion. Friction binding requires modest additional pressure, not new shocks.

The world in 2030.

The scenario the techno-optimists have been promising and the S2G report half-believes. By 2028, generative AI is doing for the physical economy what the early internet did for retail in 1999 to 2003: clumsy at first, then increasingly load-bearing, then suddenly the default.

Yield-prediction models running on hyperspectral satellite plus farm-level sensor data give large-acre operators a 12 to 18% margin lift across a full season. The Climate Corporation-shaped vendors who failed in 2014-2020 have been replaced by a new cohort that solved the data-integration problem (LLMs ate the ERP) and the deployment problem (foundation models eliminated the bespoke ML team requirement). John Deere's See & Spray and Blue River-class systems are now table stakes.

Aquaculture has been transformed. Real-time biological monitoring cut salmon mortality by 35 to 50% by 2029. Cold-chain logistics platforms eliminated 25% of seafood spoilage. The $1.5T aquaculture investment thesis the S2G report cites is visibly underway. Port-routing AI cut Hormuz-style insurance shocks. In energy, grid-edge storage plus AI-optimized dispatch unlocked tens of gigawatts of behind-the-meter capacity. Industrial AI saved 15 to 20% on industrial heat and process loads.

The model layer commoditized. By 2028 GPT-6-class capability runs on open weights. Anthropic and OpenAI went public in 2027 at sober multiples. The 4-lab oligopoly is intact but the margin structure looks like cellular telecom in 2005, not Microsoft in 1998.

What this means for S2G.

S2G's partial home field. The three-sector focus is vindicated. Clear Frontier and the bespoke-fund cohort earn excellent returns. Growth equity into the missing middle works because the missing middle is real and the operators emerge.

BUT: capital architecture innovation is not the differentiating edge here. Domain expertise is. S2G's specific advantage is sector knowledge, operator networks, and willingness to underwrite biological / operational variance. The alpha is in picking the right operators, not inventing the right vehicle.

"S2G should want this scenario, but should not over-allocate to it. The alpha here is operational, and S2G's edge in operational alpha is real but bounded."

Panel weighting: ~20-25%. Plausible, requires all four constraint-release conditions (power, geopolitics, trust, capability) to roll the right way over 3-5 years.

"Value diffuses to the physical economy AND constraints bite. The financeability gap stays open wider, longer. Capital architecture is the alpha. The joint-stock-company moment."

The world in 2030.

This is the scenario the S2G report describes. By 2028 it is increasingly visible that gen AI is producing real economic value across the physical economy (yields up, dispatch smarter, freight more efficient, monitoring real-time) AND that conventional capital markets cannot price it. The two trends together are what creates the seam where the alpha lives.

Constraints bite, but not catastrophically. Power buildout is slow but not stalled. Geopolitics holds at partial bifurcation, but a Taiwan-adjacent event in 2027 (not war, but a serious disruption) reprices insurance, lead times, and risk premia across the entire AI supply chain. The EU AI Act bites harder than expected. The trust layer forms slowly, after three high-profile AI failures (a medical AI, an agentic trading system, an autonomous fishing-quota system) created the legibility of "AI risk" the trust infrastructure needed to price against.

Meanwhile the operating reality across food, energy, and oceans is genuinely transformed. The 58-year-old farmer still didn't adopt, but his 36-year-old farm manager did, and the resulting platform-operator class (5,000 to 50,000 acres, AI-enabled, multi-revenue-stream) is a real category that needs a real capital partner.

The capital markets failed to keep up. Banks didn't underwrite the new asset class because nothing fit their templates. Project finance shops wouldn't touch operating-stage businesses below their minimum check size. Growth equity continued its retreat. The financeability gap stayed open. And got wider as economic value accelerated.

Where the alpha lived: five new capital architectures.

  • Tranched, milestone-linked operator-platform equity for ag, aquaculture, and grid-edge. The Clear Frontier model expands into 6-8 vertical-specific bespoke funds. Asset class size by 2030: $80-150B AUM.
  • Compute-capacity offtake securitization. GPU portfolios with contracted hyperscaler / neocloud offtake, financed by long-duration credit. $200-400B by 2030. The single largest white-space opportunity, currently un-served by any major fund.
  • Climate-variance-linked structured products that monetize AI-driven yield and loss reduction. Pays AI-enabled operators for variance reduction (e.g., reduced crop-insurance payouts). Nascent but on the curve.
  • Power-and-AI coupled offtake instruments linking behind-the-meter generation to AI compute demand on 10-15 year horizons. $50-100B by 2030.
  • Trust-layer financing. State-adjacent but private-market-financed institutions that provide AI verification, audit, and licensing as a utility. $20-40B by 2030. The under-developed strategy track that S2G's report did not name.

What this means for S2G.

Home field, fully vindicated. The three-strategy framework maps directly onto the asset classes that emerge.

Special Opportunities funds the asset-backed bridge financings across the missing middle. Bespoke Funds vehicles like Clear Frontier expand into 6-8 verticals, each purpose-built for an operating-economic reality conventional capital couldn't reach. Growth Equity funds the platforms once they've crossed the financeability threshold built by Special Opportunities.

"The fit-for-purpose vehicles for the AI-coupled physical economy precede the category they enable. We are now in that pre-category moment."

Panel weighting: ~20-25%. Plausible. Requires the combination of (a) constraints continuing to bite, (b) AI value continuing to materialize in physical sectors, (c) conventional capital continuing to be slow. Each is independently moderate-to-likely.

THE STRATEGIC READ

An asymmetric bet.

Across four scenarios, S2G's three-strategy framework produces a strikingly asymmetric payoff. In two scenarios the alpha thesis dissolves to sector specialism. In two scenarios the capital-architecture innovation pays off, and in one of those (Scenario D) it pays off generationally.

The strategic argument is not "will gen AI deliver?" but "how heavily should you bet on the home field, given that the downside in the wrong scenarios is small and the upside in the right scenario is uniquely large?"

"S2G should position for D, hedge for B, and tolerate A or C without distress."

A REFLECTION, NOT A RECOMMENDATION

The recognition has only just begun.

Three things become visible from these scenarios that were not visible from the report alone.

  1. S2G's worldview has a home field, Scenario D, where it is the most powerful frame available. In the other three scenarios the playbook still produces respectable returns through sector specialization. The unique market-builder alpha is conditional.
  2. The trust-and-verification layer is under-developed in the report. Adding it as a fourth strategy track would round out the architecture. Consistent with the frame, currently un-served by competitors.
  3. The asymmetric bet structure is the real S2G argument. In A and C, S2G earns sector-specialist returns regardless of AI. In B and D, additional alpha is captured through capital architecture. The risk of being wrong is small; the upside of being right is generational.

"The lesson of the last 300 years is that alpha does not go to those who wait for the market to mature, but to the market builders who recognize the gap between a new economic order and the instruments required to finance it."
